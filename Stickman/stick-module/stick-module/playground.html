How mitochondria make energy

The mitochondrion is a double-membrane-bound organelle in which oxidative phosphorylation couples the exergonic transfer of electrons along the respiratory chain to the endergonic synthesis of adenosine triphosphate, via a proton-motive force established across the inner membrane by chemiosmotic gradients.

Cells need a steady supply of energy to survive. Most of that energy is produced in small compartments called mitochondria, and the details of how they do it have been studied for more than a century.
